overview: In this project, our group of four was tasked with writing a research proposal that could be realistically pitched here at the University of Central Florida (UCF). We chose to analyze the potential benefits of utilizing artificial intelligence for student academic advising.

approach: The first step and foundation of our project was research. We researched peer-reviewed journal articles, gathering data about the successes and failures involved in implementing AI in academic advising. Next, we planned our own research process to build on existing research in the field. For example, we included conventional proposal sections describing the problem, solution, and budget. We were able to cut down on research costs by using free Copilot services provided by UCF, but these services only partially cover the total AI expenses.

revisions: While this project was initially created in a group, I made revisions individually. The key issue with our research proposal was a lack of explanation for our problem. Without strong evidence of the flaws in academic advising, our research could be perceived as unnecessary. To revise this, I strengthened the problem description with specific cited statistics. For example, the proposal now emphasizes the average time needed to schedule advising appointments. Our initial proposal also faced feasibility issues. Specifically, it was unreasonable to expect that UCF would provide access to student data. To solve this issue, the research was adjusted to rely on students manually importing their data.

learning: Writing a proposal has taught me about the benefits and feasibility of research. It is wonderful to see that universities provide funding for research projects. To fully use these opportunities, a proper research project must be designed along with a clear research proposal. Encountering feasibility issues in my research proposal has helped me strengthen my skills in planning feasible research. Working in a team has also taught me how to organize collaborative writing.
